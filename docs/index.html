How it works

Three steps, no one has to remember to report.

Today the borrower is the one who must tell the lender about a petition against itself. Drawstop checks the public record instead.

1 · Watch

Check the public record

A Chainlink CRE workflow reads The Gazette, Companies House and GLEIF on a schedule, and again whenever a borrower asks for money.

2 · Attest

Write a signed record

Each source is agreed by consensus and written on-chain as found, not found, or could not be checked. Never assumed clean.

3 · Enforce

The loan reacts

The loan contract reads that record before any money moves: drawdowns stop, repayments are held, people make the decisions.

What the loan contract does

The agreement's own terms, where the money moves.

Drawstop

New drawdowns revert while a petition or an insolvency proceeding is on record.

Repayments held

Money paid once a petition is known goes into suspense instead of out to token holders, because it may have to go back to the company's creditors.

Fails closed

A source that cannot be checked, or a record that has expired, blocks drawdowns instead of letting them through.

People decide

No automatic default. Reviews are recorded and expire, and held money can go only to the lender, the borrower or a nominated office-holder.
