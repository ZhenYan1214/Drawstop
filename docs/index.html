The gap, measured

Thirty‑six days.

The median time between a winding-up petition being presented in England and Wales, when every repayment the company makes is put at risk, and its first appearance in free open data.

Q3 2026 · 1,594 petitions · Data: The Gazette, Companies House · Not legal advice

Drawstop is a Chainlink CRE workflow that reads the public insolvency record and writes a signed status on-chain. A tokenized loan reads it and does what an LMA-style agreement already says: no new drawdowns, repayments held, and acceleration left to the lender.

§ 1The gap

Two clocks start at presentation. Neither is visible.

  1. 1Insolvency Act 1986, s.129(2): a compulsory winding-up is deemed to commence when the petition is presented.
  2. 2s.127: after commencement, any disposition of the company's property is void unless the court orders otherwise. It bites only if a winding-up order is made.
  3. 3Express Electrical Distributors Ltd v Beavis [2016] EWCA Civ 765: the payee's good faith is not enough.
  4. 4Insolvency (England and Wales) Rules 2016, r.7.10: advertisement at least seven business days after service.
  5. 5Torre Asset Funding v RBS [2013] EWHC 2670 (Ch): the agent may assume no Default has occurred unless told.
  6. 6Centrifuge governance forum, ConsolFreight CF4 pool update, August 2023.

Under the law of England and Wales, a compulsory winding-up is treated as starting the day a petition is presented.1 If a winding-up order follows, every disposition of the company's property from that day, a loan repayment included, is void unless the court validates it,2 and it does not help that the lender had no idea.3

Yet a petition first reaches free open data only when The Gazette advertises it, at least seven business days after it is served.4 Until then it sits on the court file. It is never filed at Companies House.

i. The default clock

LMA-style agreements stop new drawdowns while a Default continues. A petition gets a grace window only if it is frivolous or vexatious and dismissed within [14] days. The agent may assume nothing is wrong unless told,5 and the borrower is the one who must tell it.

ii. The s.127 clock

Runs from presentation whatever the carve-out says. Every repayment after that date is at risk if an order follows, and it has to come back from whoever received it.

Off-chain, a bank that learns of a petition usually freezes the company's account. That is bank practice, not law, and it has no equivalent for a stablecoin wallet: on-chain repayments keep flowing to token holders unless the loan contract itself stops them.

Nobody freezes a stablecoin wallet. The loan contract has to know.Token holders in one Centrifuge pool learned of a borrower's court-ordered liquidation 53 days after the order.6

§ 4The mechanism

The agreement's own terms, where the money moves.

A Chainlink CRE workflow checks three public registries on a schedule and on every drawdown request, agrees on each source, and writes a signed status. The loan contract reads it before any money moves.

Triggers

Schedule and request

  • Cron: every registered company
  • EVM log: a fresh check on every drawdown request
Chainlink CRE

Three sources, one answer each

  • The Gazette: primary, each notice confirmed
  • Companies House: status, filings
  • GLEIF: cross-check only
  • Disagreement → “could not be checked”
StatusRegistry

A signed attestation

  • As of time T, in source S, event E was found, not found, or could not be checked
  • Expires after a day
LoanFacility

The agreement, enforced

  • Drawstop and carve-out window
  • s.127 suspense for repayments
  • Reviews by a neutral arbiter
  • Acceleration: the lender's call

  1. Absence is not resolution. A petition stays live until a dismissal for the same court case is found, or a winding-up order supersedes it.
  2. “Could not be checked” is never “clean”. A failed or disputed source blocks drawdowns and says so.
  3. Humans accelerate. The contract never accelerates, enforces security or cancels the lender's commitment on its own.
  4. Reviews are narrow. Append-only, expiring, and limited to what the latest attestation shows; they cannot release suspense while a petition is live.
  5. Suspense has three exits. The lender, the borrower, or an office-holder nominated on-chain two days in advance.
  6. The agreement prevails over the code. Illustrative facility terms are drafted in the rider ↗, flagged where specialist advice is needed.

    § 7Limits

    What we do not claim.

    1. Not faster than commercial vendors, some of which see petitions before The Gazette. Drawstop changes what happens once a petition is public.
    2. “Not found” never rules out a petition presented but not yet advertised. That is a limit of free open data, not of the law: 99% of England-and-Wales petitions sit in courts the CE-File search covers, a source that could be added.
    3. A payment between presentation and advertisement cannot be stopped, only surfaced afterwards.
    4. The legal design is illustrative. The draft facility terms flag what needs specialist advice. Not legal advice.
    5. Test setup. The CRE simulator runs consensus on one node; Sepolia writes go through the simulation forwarder; the registry owner is the test lender.
    6. No user interviews yet. Demand is argued from statute, case law, LMA structure and on-chain incidents.
    7. One quarter of data, plus a two-week hold-out. Predictions registered on 17 October held on all 5 checks for 409 October petitions. 12 Q3 notices were unretrievable. Corrected on 22 October to whole calendar days: the 30-day figure is 75%, not the 79% first published.
    8. Mostly England and Wales. 10% of the petitions concern Scottish or Northern Irish companies, whose rules differ; they are kept in, which makes the figures conservative (England and Wales only: 98% after 14 days).
