## 1. Who is the Blockchain Legal Institute (BLI)?

### Takeaway
BLI is a small Maryland-based blockchain legal media, education and membership organization (with a 501(c)(3) foundation arm). It runs the hackathon together with the Maryland Blockchain Association (MDBA). It is not a law firm. No founder is named on its site. The one named officer found is Matthew Rogers (CTO of BLI, co-founder of MDBA). The 2026 edition is explicitly framed around "Youth and Legal Tech".

### Cited Findings
- BLI describes its mission as being a "global hub for blockchain-related legal resources, legislation, cases, conferences, articles and webinars". It calls itself a media and publishing company that "is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice". Mailing address: PO Box 1652, Beltsville, Maryland 20705 — [BLI About](https://bli.tools/about/)
- Membership benefits listed: research on 50 US states and 100+ nations, legal opinions, conference discounts, a referral network, CE (continuing education) credits, and access to the hackathon. BLI holds Global Summits in December and March — [BLI About](https://bli.tools/about/); [bli.tools search page](https://bli.tools/?s=winners)

No law firm is identified as an "affiliate" — [BLI About-BLI](https://bli.tools/about-bli/)

### Inferences
- The judging panel is likely to be heavy on Maryland and US legal/compliance people and educators (CE-oriented lawyers and compliance officers), not deep protocol engineers. Being legally accurate, citing statutes, and having a clear compliance story probably count for more than technical novelty.
- "Youth" is a deliberate 2026 theme (MDBA Youth Hackathon, the banner image, "data privacy for minors" listed first). A project that touches minors' privacy (COPPA, state age-appropriate design codes, age verification without doxxing) lines up directly with the organizer's stated narrative.
- Cogent Law (Allison Maffitt was a 2025 judge) and Chainlink appear as sponsors. A Chainlink CRE integration gives one build two chances: the main prize and the Chainlink bounty.

### Inferences
- The RYO-CHAN rubric language ("cause and effect", "repeatable reasoning trail", "explain rejected candidates", "practice trades", "user-defined risk limits") effectively *is* the judging criteria for the largest cash prize. Build for auditability and explainability, not trading P&L. Practice/paper trading is explicitly expected, so do not execute real trades.
- A single project can combine the RYO-CHAN agent ask with a legal/RegTech angle (for example, a KOL-promotion disclosure or market-manipulation compliance check inside the agent's reasoning trail) and a CRE workflow. That puts it in the running for the main prize, both bounties, and the "law firm" judges' interest.
- PocketChange already occupies "AI agent spending limits/delegation", so avoid a direct duplicate there.
- With 1 public BUIDL 17 days before the deadline, most submissions will probably land in the final days (2025 ended at 57 public BUIDLs). Expect roughly 30–60 entries, many of them low-effort.

### Gaps
- No official rubric, judge list, or per-place prize split for 2026 has been published (not on DoraHacks, bli.tools, bli.foundation or MDBA). The Discord/Telegram announcements could not be accessed.
- Which "blockchain law firms" will judge in 2026 is not stated. Allison Maffitt (Cogent Law) judged in 2025, and a Cogent Law logo appears on the sponsor strip.

## 4. Other legal-tech / RegTech hackathons 2024–2026: winning patterns

### Takeaway
Across legal and RegTech hackathons, winners tend to (a) solve one concrete, narrowly scoped workflow for a named user (pro-bono intake, statute reading, KYC onboarding); (b) keep personal data off-chain while proving compliance on-chain; and (c) in Chainlink and other institutional events, plug into existing compliance standards (vLEI, ISO 20022, ACE).

### Cited Findings
- **Chainlink Convergence (CRE) Hackathon, Feb 6–Mar 8, 2026.** Risk & Compliance 1st: **Aegis-Gate**, which links KYC/AML and accredited-investor checks to DeFi access "without putting personal or financial data on-chain". 2nd: ACE Sandbox (compliant cross-chain tokenized assets). Privacy 1st: SSL Stealth Settlement Layer (dark pool for tokenized RWAs). AI Agents 1st: InControl.finance (portfolio dashboard for humans and agents). 2nd: CRE Risk Router (pre-trade risk evaluation for autonomous DeFi agents). Prediction Markets 1st: TAPL. DeFi & Tokenization 1st: FlowVault — [Chainlink hackathon winners](https://chain.link/hackathon/winners). A search summary also described "Aegis Protocol V5" (on-chain execution firewall for AI agents) as a Risk & Compliance winner, which conflicts with the winners page as read; treat with caution — [search summary](https://chain.link/hackathon/winners)
- **Swift Hackathon 2025:** Chainlink won the Business Challenge with cross-border settlement of regulated digital assets using the Automated Compliance Engine, CRE, GLEIF vLEI and Swift messages. It "reduces user onboarding time by automating sanctions checks and KYC workflows". 104 entrants — [Chainlink blog](https://chain.link/blog/chainlink-wins-swift-hackathon); [Swift](https://www.swift.com/news-events/news/swift-hackathon-2025-discover-winning-teams)
- **GLEIF vLEI Hackathon (Swift + Chainlink sponsored):** LSEG won with an automated compliance framework; Clearstream was runner-up with identity verification — via [search summary of cryptonews coverage](https://cryptonews.net/news/blockchain/31926395/) (secondary)
- **Legal AI Hackathon, Toronto 2025 (Queen's Conflict Analytics Lab + Stanford CodeX):** 6-hour event. 1st place "Ace Attorney" built an intake program for pro bono organizations. Runner-up built AI trend analysis on the CBA class-action database. Prize $2,000 (Osler) plus a Spellbook subscription — [CBA National Magazine](https://nationalmagazine.ca/en-ca/articles/legal-market/legal-tech/2025/hacking-toward-legal-innovation)
- **Global Legal Hackathon:** finalists split into "business and practice of law" vs "access to justice and government solutions". Public-category winners: Decoding Law (Hong Kong ML browser plug-in that helps unrepresented litigants read legislation) and RightsNow (voice-activated legal info, New York) — [AALS](https://www.aals.org/winners-of-the-global-legal-hackathon-announced/); [HKU Business School](https://www.hkubs.hku.hk/media/school-news/bbalawllb-students-win-1-of-4-world-championship-titles-for-hackathon-invention)
- **UK online courts hackathon:** judged by a panel including High Court judges. Entries built on existing platforms "scored slightly lower in the technical coding category". The winner "went above and beyond the set challenges" — [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/online-courts-hackathon-won-colin-talking-digital-assistant-litigants/print/)
- **Old pattern, still recurring:** ETHDenver 2022 Chainlink winner IdentDeFi pulled KYC approval from an API and embedded a zk-proof in an NFT to signal on-chain compliance — [Chainlink blog](https://chain.link/blog/announcing-ethdenver-2022-hackathon-winners)

### Inferences
- The winning formula for a RegTech-flavoured Web3 entry is "compliance attestation on-chain, PII off-chain", tied to a real regulatory hook (KYC/AML, accredited investor, GDPR or COPPA). Aegis-Gate is the closest recent analogue, and a minors-privacy or age-assurance variant would not duplicate it.
- Legal-practitioner judges (High Court judges, law-firm partners) reward a crisp user story and legal correctness. Engineering judges penalize thin wrappers around existing platforms.
- Chainlink's own judges have rewarded AI-agent *risk controls* (CRE Risk Router) over agents that just trade. That fits RYO-CHAN's "reasoning trail" emphasis.

- No 2025 Stanford CodeX-only hackathon winner list found beyond the Toronto co-hosted event.

## 5. Failure modes, RYO-CHAN sponsor, and DoraHacks judging/payout mechanics

### Takeaway
RYO-CHAN is the AI mascot and meme-coin brand of the Japan-linked RYO Project crypto ecosystem, which is a listed BLI partner ("RYO Digital" thanked for supporting BLI projects). Most information about it comes from paid press releases. On DoraHacks, bounty winners are typically picked by the sponsor (in 2025 Constellation named its own "Bounty Decision Maker"), and payouts depend on the sponsor or organizer. There are documented community complaints about opaque winner selection and delays in other DoraHacks events. Legal Hack 2 explicitly reserves the right to change or withdraw prizes.

### Cited Findings
- RYO-CHAN is marketed as "an AI meme coin featuring RYO SCAN and AI Companion, designed to help users detect risks, avoid scams" — [feedbagel](https://feedbagel.com/post/the-meme-coin-space-is-evolving-communities-matter-culture-matters-but-users-are)
- The RYO Project (Japan) includes RYOPAY (a stablecoin, first use case grain-trade settlement with GRNX Global), a Global Mall e-commerce site, LIFE Wallet, and a crypto ATM network. The RYO-Chan mascot is "a real-life schnauzer mascot, powered by AI, who leads smart contract analysis and portfolio management" (June 2025 press release; self-reported) — [CoinCodex](https://coincodex.com/article/68587/ryo-project-launching-ryopay-with-first-real-world-use-case-in-the-grain-trade-industry); [Bitcoinist](https://bitcoinist.com/ryo-project-launching-ryopay-with-first-real-world-use-case-in-the-grain-trade-industry/amp/)
- RYO is listed among BLI partners, and BLI thanked "RYO Digital" for "continuing support of BLI projects" after the 2025 hackathon — [BLI About-BLI](https://bli.tools/about-bli/); [BLI post](https://bli.tools/bli-legal-tech-hackathon-global-projects-address-compliance-challenges/)
- RYO-CHAN frames its 2026 bounty as "a hands-on developer education program" in partnership with BLI and calls trades "practice trades" — [DoraHacks bounty 1380](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/bounty/1380)
- 2025 Constellation roles included Dave Berg as "Bounty Decision Maker" and Alex Brandes as "Technical Judge". In other words, sponsor staff decide the sponsor bounties — [BLI post](https://bli.tools/bli-legal-tech-hackathon-global-projects-address-compliance-challenges/)
- A DFINITY forum post alleges that the WCHL25 (ICP Hubs × DoraHacks) $30k qualification round never announced winners or disclosed recipients (a single user's allegation) — [DFINITY forum](https://forum.dfinity.org/t/wchl25-hackathon-lack-of-transparency/56602)

### Inferences
- **Likely failure modes, based on the 2025 field and other events:** a generic crypto app with a token-thin "legal" label (a large share of 2025 entries); no sponsor-technology integration (2025 cash was 100% integration bounties); no demo video or pitch (git and video were optional, but the 13 pitch-day projects got extra exposure); legal claims that are not jurisdiction-specific; and, for RYO-CHAN, an opaque "black-box" agent with no reasoning trail, or one that executes real trades.
- For payout safety: keep all communication in writing on DoraHacks or Discord, and expect bounty decisions to sit with sponsor staff (RYO-CHAN team, Chainlink DevRel) rather than BLI's law-firm judges.
- RYO-CHAN's brand is "AI for crypto safety and scam detection". An agent that flags undisclosed paid KOL promotion, pump-and-dump convergence, or scam narratives (while still producing a practice position) fits both the sponsor's brand and BLI's legal/compliance mission.
