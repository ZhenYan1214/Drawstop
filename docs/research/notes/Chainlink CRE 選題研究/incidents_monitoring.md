# Blockchain Incidents 2025–2026, Missing Controls, and the Monitoring/Response Tool Landscape

Research date: 2026-10-14. Scope: incidents Jan 2025 – Oct 2026. Older facts are marked [pre-2025]. Most figures come from security vendors and crypto media. Totals differ by tracker because each counts different things (CEX vs DeFi only, gross vs net of recoveries, whether individual thefts are included).

## 1. Top incidents 2025–2026: losses, root cause, detection/response, warning signs, missing control

### Takeaway
The biggest losses in 2025–2026 were not smart-contract bugs. They came from operational and infrastructure compromise: signer UIs, multisig pre-signing, RPC/verifier nodes, backend wallet systems. They also came from opaque off-chain counterparty risk (Stream/Elixir) and hardcoded or single-venue oracle pricing (xUSD at $1, USDe on Binance). In most cases, the warning sign was a gap between two data sources: what signers saw vs. what they signed, oracle price vs. market price, claimed backing vs. what could actually be verified. Simple cross-source checks could have flagged these gaps, and on-chain-only, contract-centric monitors mostly do not run such checks.

### Cited Findings

**Bybit, 21 Feb 2025, about $1.45–1.5B (largest crypto theft ever)**
- CertiK puts the Bybit loss at $1,447,063,421. Without Bybit, 2025 would have shown a net decrease in stolen funds vs. 2024 — [CertiK Hack3d 2025 via Blockmedia](https://www.blockmedia.co.kr/research-english/1024739); [CertiK Hack3d 2025](https://certik.com/blog/hack3d-the-web3-security-report-2025)
- Root cause: a supply-chain attack on Safe{Wallet}. Sygnia (as relayed by secondary coverage) found a Safe developer's macOS workstation compromised by social engineering on 4 Feb 2025. The attackers then operated inside Safe's AWS from 5–17 Feb. The app.safe.global JS was replaced with malicious code on 19 Feb 2025 at 15:29:25 UTC (Verichains). It targeted Bybit's next transaction and swapped the payload for a delegatecall to an attacker contract. — [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/02/bybit-hack-traced-to-safewallet-supply.html); [Cyber Security News (Sygnia summary)](https://cybersecuritynews.com/bybit-hack-sophisticated-multi-stage-attack); [SlowMist analysis](https://slowmist.medium.com/bybits-1-5-billion-theft-unveiled-safe-wallet-front-end-code-tampered-84b78f0fa9c2)
- Signers "blindly signed" without checking full details on the hardware device (Chainalysis' Andrew Fierman). The FBI attributed the attack to TraderTraitor/Lazarus (DPRK). — [CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/news/behind-the-bybit-crypto-theft); [Rekt "Not So Safe"](https://rekt.news/not-so-safe)
- Missing control (inference): independent simulation and decoding of the payload before signing (delegatecall to an unknown contract on a cold-wallet Safe), checked against a source other than the compromised UI.

**Cetus (Sui), 22 May 2025, about $223–225M**
- Root cause: a faulty left-shift overflow check in a third-party math library, a different issue from the MAX_U64 bug flagged in earlier audits. The attacker added huge liquidity with about 1 token unit and drained the pools. — [Cointelegraph / Dedaub post-mortem](https://cointelegraph.com/news/dedaub-cetus-hack-post-mortem); [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/analysts-uncover-cause-of-cetus-hack/); [Cyfrin](https://www.cyfrin.io/blog/inside-the-223m-cetus-exploit-root-cause-and-impact-analysis)
- Response: Sui validators froze about $162M. Validators holding over 90% of stake voted to move the funds to a multisig for restitution. Critics called this centralization. Cetus also offered the hacker a $6M bounty with a promise not to contact law enforcement. — [Chainflow](https://chainflow.io/chainflows-official-response-to-the-cetus-exploit-and-sui-governance-vote/); [CertiK H1 2025 via Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/otal-hacks-down-q2-after-record-losses-2025-h1)
- Bybit plus Cetus made up about $1.78B, or 72%, of H1 2025 losses — [Crowdfund Insider / CertiK](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/07/243940-hacking-losses-surge-thanks-to-bybit-cetus-certik/)

**10 Oct 2025 crash / USDe depeg on Binance: about $19B in liquidations (largest liquidation event ever)**
- $19B (Coinglass: $19.16B) liquidated after the tariff announcement. — [CoinShares](https://coinshares.com/insights/knowledge/billions-in-liquidations-what-happened/); [CoinGecko explainer](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)
- Binance priced collateral from its own order book rather than a multi-venue index. USDe hit about $0.65 on Binance while staying near $1 elsewhere, which triggered USDe-collateral margin calls. Hyperliquid's 8-venue composite oracle filtered out the anomaly. (Research Square preprint, not peer reviewed.) — [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9459584/v1)
- Ethena: mint/redeem had no downtime and processed more than $1B in redemptions within hours. — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/newsletter/defi-daily/the-ultimate-10-10-crash-autopsy)
- Disputed: CZ called the claims that Binance caused the crash "far-fetched". Haseeb Qureshi said Binance's oracle was poorly implemented. OKX's CEO (31 Jan 2026) blamed Binance's 12% USDe yield campaign. Claims that someone deliberately manipulated the market remain unverified. — [Yahoo/CZ](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/cz-pushes-back-claims-binance-103600865.html); [The Defiant "Is Binance to Blame?"](https://thedefiant.io/newsletter/defi-daily/is-binance-to-blame)
- Missing control (inference): checking venue prices against a cross-venue reference, with automatic pause or haircut when one venue diverges.

**Stream Finance xUSD → Elixir deUSD → USDX contagion, Nov 2025, about $93M direct loss and about $285M interconnected debt**
- Timeline: on 10 Oct 2025 the ETH drawdown liquidated the external manager's positions. The loss stayed invisible on-chain for about 25 days because xUSD still read $1 in the lending markets. On 4 Nov, Stream disclosed a $93M loss, halted deposits and redemptions, and hired counsel (Perkins Coie). xUSD fell about 77% to about $0.26. On 6 Nov, deUSD fell about 98% to about $0.015 and Elixir wound it down. USDX broke below $0.60. On 8 Nov, about $285M of interconnected debt was mapped and about $1B left DeFi yield products within a week. On 8 Dec, Stream's entity filed a lawsuit alleging the manager covered personal losses (contested). — [Pharos case study](https://pharos.watch/learn/case-studies/stream-elixir-contagion-2025/); [BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2025/11/08/m-defi-contagion/)
- Structural red flags: no multisig over capital, no disclosed custody, no live attestation of backing, xUSD looped through lending markets. About 65% of deUSD backing (about $68M) was lent to Stream via private Morpho vaults where Stream was the only borrower, using xUSD as collateral. That made the dependency recursive. — [Pharos](https://pharos.watch/learn/case-studies/stream-elixir-contagion-2025/); [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/elixir-deusd-stablecoin-collapse-stream-finance-loss/)
- Hardcoded oracle: Morpho, Euler, and Silo vaults priced xUSD at a fixed $1, so liquidations never fired. Borrowers kept the USDC and lenders were left with bad debt. — [Pharos](https://pharos.watch/learn/case-studies/stream-elixir-contagion-2025/)
- Curator losses: TelosC was the largest creditor at about $124M (Pharos). MEV Capital had about $34M of impacted assets, with depositor haircuts of 3.5% (Ethereum vault) and 12% (Arbitrum vault). Re7 had $14.65M (xUSD/USDT0 on Plasma) plus $12.75M (sdeUSD/deUSD on Euler/Morpho). Re7 said its due diligence had already identified "centralized counterparty risk" in xUSD before listing it, and it reportedly threatened legal action against a depositor "whistleblower". — [Protos](https://protos.com/stream-finance-meltdown-winners-and-losers-in-defi-risk-curator-reckoning/); [Protos follow-up](https://protos.com/high-yields-to-haircuts-has-defi-learned-anything-from-yield-vault-collapse/); [Morpho forum: MEV Capital USDC vault loss](https://forum.morpho.org/t/usdc-vault-loss-mev-capital/2078)
- Missing control (inference): continuous checks of each curator vault's collateral, covering oracle type (hardcoded vs. market), divergence between market and oracle price, concentration in a single counterparty or borrower, recursive collateral loops, and whether the collateral issuer has any verifiable attestation. Plus an alert and evidence trail when an issuer stops publishing data or halts redemptions.

**Drift Protocol (Solana), 1 Apr 2026, about $285M (one of the two largest DeFi losses of H1 2026)**
- Drained in about 12 minutes. Attackers got 2 of 5 Security Council signers to pre-sign malicious admin transactions, using Solana durable nonces so those transactions could wait for weeks. There was no timelock on the admin actions. The attackers created a fake token (CVT) with about $500 of Raydium liquidity, wash-traded it to build a price history the oracle would accept, listed it as collateral, disabled withdrawal safeguards, and drained real assets. Devices were compromised via a malicious repo and a fake TestFlight app. The social engineering began in fall 2025 with in-person conference meetings, and the operation is attributed to DPRK. — [BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/drift-protocol-incident-multisig-governance-compromise-via-durable-nonce-exploitation); [Nexus Mutual incident report](https://nexusmutual.io/blog/drift-protocol-incident-report); [QuillAudits](https://www.quillaudits.com/blog/hack-analysis/drift-protocol-multisig-exploit); [4Pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/reflections-on-the-drift-protocol-exploit)
- Reports say durable-nonce accounts were created around 23 Mar, which was an on-chain warning sign about a week before the drain (sources conflict on the dates). — [Coin360](https://coin360.com/news/drift-protocol-exploit-285m-nonce-attack-solana)
- Freeze response: ZachXBT said the attacker bridged funds through Circle's CCTP for 6 hours and no USDC was frozen. Circle's CEO said Circle freezes only on a court order or a law-enforcement request. Tether provided about $127.5–147M of loans and grants for Drift's recovery. — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/04/17/tether-127-5-million-drift-circle-freeze-hacked-funds/); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/tether-gives-drift-147-million-to-help-address-crypto-hack)
- Missing control (inference): monitors for privileged-key and governance actions (new durable nonce accounts tied to signers, pre-signed admin transactions, new collateral listings with thin liquidity), timelocks, and automatic evidence packages to support freeze requests. Issuers like Circle want a formal legal basis, so the speed of producing a legally usable request becomes the bottleneck.

**KelpDAO rsETH via LayerZero, 18 Apr 2026, about $292M**
- 116,500 rsETH was minted with nothing backing it, from a forged cross-chain message. The DVN used a 1-of-1 configuration. LayerZero says the RPC nodes its DVN relied on were poisoned and the clean nodes DDoSed. Researchers describe it as a perimeter breach: two nodes' op-geth binaries were swapped and served forged data only to the DVN's IPs while answering LayerZero's own monitoring honestly. The attacker posted the rsETH as collateral on Aave V3 and borrowed WETH, and Aave froze its rsETH markets. Attributed to DPRK (TraderTraitor). — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/04/20/kelp-dao-claims-layerzero-s-default-settings-are-what-actually-caused-the-usd290-million-disaster); [Rekt](https://rekt.news/kelpdao-rekt); [Whale Alert story](https://whale-alert.io/stories/b5a8019a775d36/LayerZero-Kelps-single-11-DVN-enabled-293M-rsETH-exploit-Ethereum-researcher-urges-validity-proofs)
- Kelp disputes the blame and says LayerZero's quickstart and default configuration were 1/1. Kelp moved to 4 attestors and 64 confirmations, and is migrating to Chainlink CCIP. — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/kelp-dao-accuses-layerzero-of-deflecting-blame-for-usd300m-bridge-hack); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/401060/kelp-dao-aave-resume-rseth)
- Missing control (inference): a supply-integrity check across chains (total bridged-out supply ≤ locked supply, a Proof-of-Reserve / Secure Mint style invariant) that runs independently of the bridge's own verifier. Plus automatic freezing of the collateral on lending markets that accept it.

**Bitget, detected 24 Sep 2026, about $387.5M (largest CEX hack of 2026)**
- The attacker compromised a backend wallet system and spoofed transaction data, so Bitget's own authorization approved the outflows. No private keys were stolen and cold wallets were safe. The initial figure was $351.6M, revised to $387.5M after omitted ZEC and TRON balances were added. About 103M XRP (about $157M) was laundered via THORChain into BTC. Chainalysis links most of it to DPRK, taking 2026 DPRK theft above $1B. Bitget's User Protection Fund (above $464M) covers users. Withdrawals were frozen while trading continued. Mandiant and SlowMist are investigating. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379350/bitget-hack-387m-what-happened-why-north-korea-suspect); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/hacks/chainalysis-links-387-million-bitget-hack-to-north-korea); [The Record](https://therecord.media/crypto-ceo-accuses-north-korea-of-387-million-theft)
- Note the revised loss figure: the first disclosure was incomplete, which is a gap in incident-reporting accuracy.

**Other 2026 incidents**
- Liquid Network (Blockstream), 6 Sep 2026: about $319M in BTC drained via a validator-software bug that minted unbacked tokens. About 85% (about $272M) was returned. TRM calls it the largest theft of 2026 by their count. — [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/2026s-biggest-hack-to-date-attackers-drained-usd-319-million-in-bitcoin-from-liquid-network-then-returned-85-of-funds)

**RWA / tokenized private credit**
- I found no well-sourced, large on-chain RWA default or NAV write-down in 2025–2026. On-chain private credit is about $6.99B distributed / $35.94B represented (RWA.xyz, Aug 2026), and Figure's HELOCs are about 60% of that. Recorded defaults are mostly [pre-2025]: Maple about $36M (2022) and Goldfinch about $18M. ### Inferences
- A common pattern: for Stream (25 days), Drift (nonce accounts about a week early), and Bitget (incomplete first disclosure), information existed before or during the incident but sat off-chain or across sources nobody was linking up.
- The 2025–2026 lesson is less "detect the exploit transaction faster" and more "notice when backing, signer intent, or pricing assumptions stop matching reality". Examples: a reserve or attestation feed going stale, an oracle diverging from market, a bridged supply invariant breaking, a privileged-action anomaly.
- Most large-loss vectors (key/signer compromise, about 76% of H1 2026 losses per TRM) cannot be stopped by a contract-level guardian once the signature is valid. What remains possible is downstream containment (freezing collateral, issuer freezes) and fast evidence and reporting. That favors a response/evidence workflow over yet another detector.

### Gaps
- No primary Drift or LayerZero post-mortem text was retrieved directly; details come via BlockSec, Nexus Mutual, CoinDesk, and Rekt.
- Exact detection-to-response times are mostly unavailable, except Drift's 12-minute drain and Circle's 6-hour non-freeze.
- No sourced 2025–2026 on-chain RWA default of material size was found. Treat the RWA risk as forward-looking.
- No official Morpho or Euler post-mortem on Stream was found.

## 2. Aggregate loss statistics (2025, H1 2026, YTD 2026)

### Takeaway
2025 was the worst year for dollar losses because of Bybit: about $3.35B per CertiK (broad definition), with about $2.0B stolen by DPRK per Chainalysis. 2026 has fewer dollars stolen in H1 (about $972M per TRM) but a record number of incidents (207 in H1, Q2 the most-hacked quarter on record). Losses concentrated in a few DPRK operational compromises, and September 2026 (Bitget, Liquid) was the worst month.

### Cited Findings
- CertiK 2025 full-year total: $3,352,850,816, vs. $2,446,285,251 in 2024 (+37%). Supply-chain attacks caused $1.45B. Phishing had the most incidents (248). Ethereum had 310 incidents and $1.698B in losses. — [Blockmedia/CertiK](https://www.blockmedia.co.kr/research-english/1024739)
- CertiK H1 2025: about $2.47B gross, $2.29B net of freezes and returns. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/otal-hacks-down-q2-after-record-losses-2025-h1)
- Chainalysis 2026 Crypto Crime Report (covering 2025): illicit volume at least $154B. DPRK stole $2.02B in 2025 (+51% YoY, per a summary). — [Chainalysis on X](https://x.com/chainalysis/status/2029539952128622847); [Libertify summary](https://www.libertify.com/interactive-library/chainalysis-crypto-crime-report/)
- Chainalysis mid-2026: violent "wrench" attacks extracted more than $30M in H1 2026 (2025 was a record $58M), from 46 attacks. I found no general Chainalysis mid-year hack-total report. — [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/violent-crypto-wrench-attacks-2026/)
- TRM H1 2026: 207 incidents (a record, vs. 83 in H1 2025), about $972M stolen (vs. about $2.3B), median loss about $219k. ### Inferences
- Rising incident counts with falling median losses suggest a long tail of small protocols without dedicated security teams. That is the market a cheap, self-hosted CRE workflow could serve, rather than tier-1 protocols that already pay Hypernative or Hexagate.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Immunefi or SlowMist 2025 / H1 2026 totals.
- Stream/Elixir ($93M + contagion) and the 10/10 liquidations ($19B) are usually not counted as "hacks" in these totals, so tracker figures understate losses from risk and transparency failures.

## 3. Monitoring and automated response tool landscape, and its gaps

### Takeaway
Real-time on-chain threat detection with automated pause is a crowded, consolidating market: Hypernative, Hexagate (Chainalysis), BlockSec Phalcon, and Forta. OpenZeppelin Defender's hosted service was sunset on 1 Jul 2026. Parameter-level risk management (Chaos Labs Edge, LlamaRisk, Gauntlet) is in turmoil after Chaos Labs and Gauntlet left Aave. Chainlink PoR / Secure Mint covers reserves vs. supply. The gaps are off-chain and cross-source signals (attestation staleness, issuer filings and halts, curator collateral quality) and post-incident evidence, reporting, and freeze-request workflows. No major vendor productizes those.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenZeppelin Defender**: phase-out announced June 2025 with sign-ups closed, final shutdown 1 Jul 2026. Users can self-host the open-source Relayer and Monitor. Keys cannot be exported from Defender's KMS, and there is no open-source replacement for Deploy. KeeperHub markets itself as a managed alternative. — [OpenZeppelin announcement](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/doubling-down-on-open-source-and-phasing-out-defender); [Sunset FAQ](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/defender-sunset-faq); [OZ forum](https://forum.openzeppelin.com/t/defender-sunset-no-migration-path-for-create2-deterministic-deployments-via-hardhat-upgrades/45272); [KeeperHub migration guide](https://docs.keeperhub.com/guides/defender-migration)
- **Hypernative**: ML anomaly detection over on-chain and off-chain data, more than 200 risk types, automated blocking, Security Oracle / Screener. Pricing is not public (enterprise sales). — [Chainstack listing](https://chainstack.com/ja/marketplace/applications/hypernative/); [crypto-fundraising profile](https://crypto-fundraising.info/projects/hypernative/)
- **Hexagate → Chainalysis**: acquisition around Dec 2024 for about $60M (industry estimate). Hexagate claims it detected all known hacks over two years, more than 98% before they occurred, and that its customers saved more than $1B (self-reported). Chainalysis framed the deal as moving "from investigation into prevention". — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/skfy4vehye); [Ynet](https://ynetnews.com/business/article/s1yadtlhkx); [Architect Partners PDF](https://architectpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Chainalysis-_-Hexagate-MA-Alert.pdf)
- **BlockSec Phalcon**: real-time monitoring plus automated interception. It claims more than 20 hacks blocked and more than $15M saved [pre-2025 figure, 2024 blog]. — [BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/enhancing-web3-security-exploring-the-top-5-security-monitoring-platforms-in-2024_ZH)
- **Forta**: a community detection-bot network. No authoritative 2026 status was found. — [AlphaGrowth](https://www.alphagrowth.io/forta)
- **Chaos Labs Edge Risk Oracles**: automated rate, cap, and PT parameters for Aave, which Chaos claimed secured more than $5B (2025). Chaos Labs left Aave in Apr 2026 (it cited budget and V4 workload; Aave says Chaos wanted to be sole provider and replace Chainlink oracles). LlamaRisk is taking over, with automated alerts on oracle deviation, utilization, liquidation cascades, and parameter anomalies, and it argues for protocol-owned risk infrastructure. Gauntlet left Aave earlier and became a Morpho curator. — [Chaos Labs](https://chaoslabs.xyz/posts/aave-integrates-chaos-labs-edge-risk-oracles); [Aave forum: Chaos renewal](https://governance.aave.com/t/chaos-labs-x-aave-dao-early-renewal-proposal/22346); [Aave forum: LlamaRisk continuity](https://governance.aave.com/t/llamarisk-ensuring-continuity-of-aaves-risk-management/24397); [BlockEden (Chaos exit)](https://blockeden.xyz/de/blog/2026/04/18/chaos-labs-exits-aave-5m-retention-defi-risk-management-crisis/); [Blockworks (Gauntlet→Morpho)](https://blockworks.com/news/gauntlet-leaves-aave-risk-curator-morpho)
- **Chainlink Proof of Reserve / Secure Mint / SmartData**: PoR feeds for stablecoins, wrapped assets, and RWAs. Secure Mint reverts a mint when reserves < supply + mint amount. SmartData embeds reserves and NAV. An unverified claim says there are more than 40 PoR feeds / 56 projects / more than $17B verified (mid-2026). — [Chainlink PoR](https://chain.link/solutions/proof-of-reserve); [Secure Mint blog](https://chain.link/blog/secure-mint); [SmartData](https://chain.link/smartdata); [Spark research (unverified stats)](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-real-time-attestation-chainlink)
- **Existing hackathon prior art (competition risk)**: SentinelFi, a Chainlink hackathon project, already computes a reserve/supply health ratio, so a plain "reserve health check" is taken. — [chain.link/hack-26/projects/sentinelfi](https://chain.link/hack-26/projects/sentinelfi)
- **Chain-level tracing and coordination**: TRM Beacon Network (more than 70 members) says it cuts response from days to minutes once attacker addresses are known. — [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/h1-2026-crypto-hacks-reach-record-high-as-losses-fall-below-usd-1-billion)
- **Curator UX gap**: Morpho's listing policy says risk is "fully externalized to curators", with interface warnings "for convenience only". Critics say UIs treat conservative and aggressive curators the same, and that Morpho's frontend whitelist included high-risk xUSD vaults, implying endorsement. Morpho Vaults v2 puts curator actions behind timelocks, and Vaults 1.1 suggests reserve funds. — [Morpho listing policy](https://docs.morpho.org/get-started/resources/interface-listing-policy/); [Morpho integrator best practices](https://docs.morpho.org/developers/earn/vault-ux/best-practices/); [Protos](https://protos.com/high-yields-to-haircuts-has-defi-learned-anything-from-yield-vault-collapse/)

### Inferences
- **Gap A, off-chain backing integrity for yield-bearing synthetic dollars and curator collateral.** PoR covers custodial reserves that have an attestor. Stream had no attestor at all. What could be monitored was the absence and staleness of disclosures, oracle type (hardcoded), oracle-vs-DEX price gaps, borrower concentration, and recursive loops. A CRE workflow could combine HTTP data (issuer transparency pages, attestation PDFs and their timestamps, redemption status, social and forum posts) with on-chain reads (Morpho/Euler market configs, oracle addresses, utilization). It could score each vault's collateral and trigger alerts or curator-side actions (e.g., queueing a cap reduction under the Vault v2 timelock).
- **Gap B, post-incident evidence and legal-response automation.** No vendor in this survey productizes this. Circle requires a court order or law-enforcement request, Tether acts within hours, and DORA's 4-hour clock applies to CASPs (see section 4). A CRE workflow could, on a triggered incident, snapshot the relevant chain state with block numbers and hashes, run consensus across DONs, anchor a hash of the evidence bundle on-chain, and pre-fill freeze-request and regulator-notification templates. That is a distinctive legal-tech angle that does not overlap with SentinelCRE (detection/guardian) or Risk Router.
- **Gap C, cross-chain supply invariant independent of the bridge verifier (KelpDAO).** This is partly covered by Chainlink PoR / Secure Mint and CCIP rate limits, and therefore less differentiated.
- Enterprise vendors are opaque on pricing and aimed at tier-1 protocols. The Defender sunset left smaller teams without a cheap hosted monitor and relayer, which is room for an open, DON-verified workflow.

### Gaps
- No pricing found for Hypernative, Hexagate, Phalcon, Cyvers, or Blockaid.
- I did not research Tenderly Alerts, Cyvers, Blockaid, or Chainlink SVR specifically.
- I did not verify whether SentinelCRE or CRE Risk Router cover curator-vault collateral or evidence packaging. The parent researcher should check their repos.

## 4. Post-incident legal/regulatory obligations, freezing, and curator-transparency demands

### Takeaway
Regulated CASPs in the EU face DORA Art. 19's tight clock: initial notice 4 hours after classifying an incident as major, and no later than 24 hours after detection. Freezing stolen stablecoins depends on the issuer's policy. Tether freezes proactively, while Circle needs a court order or law-enforcement request, so attackers had a 6-hour CCTP window in the Drift case. Users' demand for curator transparency after Stream is loud, but no formal Morpho/Euler disclosure standard was found. Together this points to a real, unaddressed need for tooling that automatically assembles a timestamped, verifiable incident record usable for regulators, issuers, insurers, and creditors.

### Cited Findings
- DORA applies to CASPs authorized under MiCA. Major ICT incidents must be reported to the national competent authority (Art. 19). The initial notification is due within 4 hours of classification and no later than 24 hours after detection. The intermediate report is commonly cited as 72 hours after the initial one and the final report about 1 month later; one source conflicts (24h / 5 business days), so check the RTS/ITS. Clients must be notified when their financial interests are affected. — [Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/dora-incident-reporting.html); [FD Capital](https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/ict-incident-reporting-dora-timelines-templates/); [sota.io guide](https://sota.io/blog/eu-dora-art19-major-ict-incident-reporting-4h-24h-5day-developer-guide)
- Circle's CEO says Circle will not freeze without a formal legal basis (court order or law enforcement). Tether froze about $344M at US law-enforcement request in Apr 2026, and was criticized for giving no detail. A Bitquery study claims Tether keeps $0.59 per dollar if it freezes an address within a week, but only $0.12 if it waits more than 6 months (methodology unverified). — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/04/17/tether-127-5-million-drift-circle-freeze-hacked-funds/); [Lider Media](https://en.lider.media/2026/04/29/tether-freezes-stablecoins-worth-344-million/); [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/tether-blacklist-audit)
- Cetus offered the hacker a bounty with a promise not to contact law enforcement or disclose the hacker's identity, which raises an evidence-handling tension. — [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/analysts-uncover-cause-of-cetus-hack/)
- After Stream: lawsuits were filed (Stream entity, 8 Dec 2025), Elixir ran a claims process for 1:1 redemption, and Re7 threatened a depositor whistleblower. These show creditor-claims and evidence disputes playing out with no shared factual record. — [Pharos](https://pharos.watch/learn/case-studies/stream-elixir-contagion-2025/); [Protos](https://protos.com/stream-finance-meltdown-winners-and-losers-in-defi-risk-curator-reckoning/)
- Bitget revised its loss from $351.6M to $387.5M after omitted assets were found, so the first public disclosure was inaccurate. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379350/bitget-hack-387m-what-happened-why-north-korea-suspect)
- Insurance: Nexus Mutual published a Drift incident report, an example of cover assessment that relies on public reconstruction. — [Nexus Mutual](https://nexusmutual.io/blog/drift-protocol-incident-report)

### Inferences
- A "CRE Incident Evidence & Notification" workflow would turn on-chain detection into legally usable outputs: a DON-attested state snapshot, a hash anchored on-chain, a freeze-request packet for each stablecoin issuer, a draft DORA initial notification, a draft claims/creditor notice, and an insurer claim. That fits the BLI Legal Tech framing, and none of the tools surveyed offers it.
- A second candidate fits the "automated risk monitoring" bounty example: a curator-vault "opaque collateral" monitor that scores off-chain backing disclosure (attestation freshness, custody, redemption status) together with on-chain oracle and market data. It could write a risk attestation on-chain that vault integrators and curators read. It answers the transparency demand after Stream and differs from PoR (which needs an attestor) and from SentinelFi (a supply/reserve ratio only).

- I found no formal regulator statement (ESMA, SEC, etc.) specifically demanding curator-vault disclosures after Stream.
- Insurance claim timelines and evidence requirements (Nexus Mutual, Sherlock, etc.) were not researched in depth.
